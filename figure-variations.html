Rig design review, round two

The head has to say where the camera is

One facing angle cannot tell you which way a body is turned, because it never says where you are standing. The same angle means “seen from the side” on a crunch and “seen from the front” on a side-lying leg lift — which is exactly why those two looked the same.

So every option below draws three cameras differently:

  • Profilethe side of the face: one eye, nose and mouth in profile, the bob squared off behind the jaw
  • Facing youthe front of the face: two eyes, a symmetric bob that widens at the jaw
  • Overheadabove the body: two eyes and a mat under the figure — or, in the last option, the back of the head and no face at all

Judge them small. This is the size they run at on a phone mid-class.

Option 01

As it ships

For comparison. One wedge on the outside of the head, the same in every view.

Option 02

Bob and arcs

A carré bob that starts behind the brow and runs past the jaw, plus a brow, nose and mouth drawn as arcs. Profile views get one eye; the views where the face is turned toward you get two, which is by itself the difference between the two cameras.

Option 03

Bob, arcs and chest

The same head with a chest curve on the front of the torso. In a profile view the chest points where the face points; seen from the front it sits symmetrically, so the torso says which camera you are looking through too.

Option 04

Bob, chest and body shape

Adds a waist and wider hips. Three shapes now disagree in a useful way between cameras: in profile the body is a curve with the bust on one edge, head-on it is a symmetric hourglass.

Option 05

Back of the head from above

Everything as above, except the overhead view shows the back of the head - all hair, no face. Looking down at a plank you would see the back of someone's head, and no face at all is the least ambiguous thing a drawing can say.

The camera is the new idea, and it is the part worth deciding first — it needs one field per pose in src/rig/rigs.ts, which the facing angle can mostly be derived into. Everything else here is a painter in src/rig/frame.ts that every pose picks up at once.
